# Memo: Where the store loses buyers, and what to do about it

**Data:** every view, add-to-cart, and purchase on the site, October–November 2019
(5.3 million shoppers). Full analysis: [README.md](README.md).

## What we found

1. **People come back, but they don't buy.** About 1 in 5 new shoppers returns
   in any given week. Only about 1 in 10 of those returners buys anything that
   week. The gap is in converting visits into purchases, not in getting
   people to visit.

2. **November's drop in conversion was mostly the mid-November promotion,
   not a broken checkout.** The share of carts that turned into a purchase
   fell from 53% in October to 39% in November. Shoppers drawn in by the
   Nov 15–17 promotion added a lot to cart but bought only 27% of the time.
   Without those three days, November converts at 47%, which accounts for
   more than half of the drop.

3. **Smartphones are where the most sales slip away.** 5.7 million visits
   looked at a smartphone and didn't buy one, more than any other product
   category.

4. **Newer shoppers are just as loyal as earlier ones.** Earlier shoppers
   look more loyal only because we watched them longer. Given the same 12
   days, every group comes back to buy again at about the same rate (17–20%).

## What we recommend

1. **Report promotion-period conversion separately.** A promotion that
   brings in browsers will always make conversion look worse. Report it on
   its own so a successful traffic event isn't mistaken for a site problem.
2. **Investigate the ~6 points of decline that the promotion doesn't
   explain.** Conversion still dropped from 53% to 47% outside the promotion.
   Check pricing, stock-outs, and site changes in November. This is the one
   number that may be a real problem.
3. **Focus conversion work on smartphones.** It has the largest volume of
   lost sales, so a small improvement there is worth more than a large one in
   a niche category.
4. **Target returning browsers.** Users who come back but don't buy (about 9
   in 10 returners) are the largest reachable group, through reminders,
   saved-cart prompts, or price-drop alerts.

## What we're not sure about

- **Cause.** This is behavioral data, not an experiment. The promotion is the
  best explanation for November, but it isn't proven, and we can't see
  marketing spend or which channels brought people in.
- **The leftover ~6 points.** We know it isn't the promotion or a change in
  what people shopped for. We don't know what it is.
- **Long-term loyalty.** Two months of data shows at most five weeks of return
  behavior. Whether loyalty differs over months is unknown.
- **The earliest "new" shoppers.** Some may be existing customers, since the
  data has no history before October 1. Their numbers are flagged rather than
  dropped.
